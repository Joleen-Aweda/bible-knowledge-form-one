Renewal of the covenant, change of names and circumcion

When Abraham (Abram) was ninety-nine (99) years old, the Lord appeared to him and said, “I am God Almighty. Walk before me and be blameless. And I will make my covenant between me and you, and will make you exceedingly numerous” (Genesis 17:2). Then Abraham fell on his face and God said, “Behold my covenant is with you, and you shall be the father of a multitude of nations. No longer shall your name be Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you the “father of multitude of nations” (Genesis 17:3-5). As a condition for the covenant God commanded Abraham and all male children to be circumcised as a sign of distinguishing the people of God from others. Ishmael was 13 years when he was circumcised.

God said, “As for Sarai your wife, you shall not call her name “Sarai”, but “Sarah” shall be her name. I will bless her, and moreover; I will give you a son by her; I will bless her and she shall be the mother of nations.” Then Abraham fell on his face and laughed. He was hundred years old. He said, “Shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?” (Genesis 17:17). Then, Abraham said, “O that Ishmael might live in thy sight? God said, “No, but Sarah your wife shall bear you a son and you shall call his name Isaac” (Genesis 17:15).

“Abram” means “Exalted Father,” whereas, “Abraham” means father of multitude of nations. On the other hand, “Sarah means mother of multitude of nations.

From Abraham and his descendants, God demanded obedience and righteousness. Furthermore, the external sign of the covenant was demonstrated through circumcision. God directed that every male person at the age of 8 days, must be circumcised. In compliance to this requirement, Abraham was circumcised at the age of 99 years, and Ishmael was circumcised at the age of 13 years.
